Browse PST and OST files without Outlook

Open a .pst or .ost archive, browse its folders and messages, and export them to EML, MBOX, CSV or PDF — all inside your browser.

Files stay on your device · no upload · no tracking

Supported PST and OST variants

  • ANSI PST (Outlook 97–2002) and Unicode PST (Outlook 2003 and later).
  • OST offline caches including the 4 KB-page format written by Outlook 2013 and newer.
  • Folders, sub-folders, emails, meeting requests, attachments and messages attached to messages.

Not supported: password-protected or “high encryption” PST files, and badly corrupted files (try your mail program’s repair tool first).

Exporting a PST

  1. Open the file and pick a folder on the left (or search across everything).
  2. Choose the scope — current folder or all loaded messages — and a format.
  3. EML (.zip) keeps one standard file per message, organised by folder; MBOX can be imported into Thunderbird (ImportExportTools NG) or Apple Mail; CSV gives a spreadsheet of date, sender, recipients, subject and attachment names; Contacts (CSV) lists every address with message counts.

Size and speed

The archive is read into memory, then folders are indexed; message bodies and attachments are decoded only when you open or export them. A 15 MB test archive with 70 messages indexes in well under a second on a laptop. For multi-gigabyte PST files use a desktop browser with plenty of free memory.

PST questions

Is my PST uploaded anywhere?
No. The page has no backend. You can verify this in your browser’s developer tools: opening a file creates no network requests.
Why are some sender addresses shown as long Exchange paths?
Messages sent inside an Exchange organisation store an internal (X.500) address. When the SMTP address is present in the file it is used instead.
